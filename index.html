Propulsion Physics

Comparative Electric Thruster Architectures

Spacecraft electric propulsion utilizes electrostatic, electrothermal, or electromagnetic fields to achieve unprecedented specific impulse values compared to chemical rockets.

01

Electrothermal

Uses electrical energy to heat a propellant (via Resistojet or Arcjet elements). Increases specific impulse over cold gas systems, delivering higher thrust density with specific impulses typically under 1,000 seconds.

Isp: < 1,000 s High Thrust
02

Electrostatic (Ion)

Ionizes propellants (Xenon, Argon, Cesium) and accelerates them through high-voltage electrostatic grids with downstream electron neutralization. Delivers high Isp (3,000–5,000 s) at micro-to-milli-Newton thrust levels.

Isp: 3,000 - 5,000 s High Isp
03

Electromagnetic

Generates dense plasma accelerated via Lorentz forces and electromagnetic field gradients. Achieves up to 100x greater thrust density than electrostatic thrusters while maintaining high Isp for deep-space orbital maneuvers.

Isp: 2,000 - 6,000 s Optimal Hybrid
